Tree rules (depth 4)
Naive Bayes multiplies each clue's evidence as if clues were independent: fast, but over-confident when clues overlap. SVM finds the widest "street" between classes; a radial kernel bends it and a large C hugs the training data (overfit risk). KNN and SVM need scaled inputs; trees and Naive Bayes do not.

Part B - Predict the performance RATING (ordinal, multi-class)

The target now has ordered classes (1 = needs improvement ... 5 = outstanding); only hires who stayed 12 months have a rating. Binary methods are stretched: logistic regression → one equation per rating (softmax); KNN → neighbours vote; Naive Bayes → highest posterior; SVM → one-vs-one voting between every pair of ratings; tree → each leaf holds the rating mix. Judge models by the size of their errors, not only whether they err.

Why shuffling channels raises ROI and cuts cost
  1. Channels cost very different amounts for the same grade. Moving a hire from a dearer channel to a cheaper one that delivers equal quality saves the difference - the cost bridge shows it.
  2. Channels deliver different quality and staying power. A hire who stays and performs creates more first-year value after salary. Moving hires to such channels lifts net contribution even if the fees are similar - the ROI bridge shows it.
  3. The right channel depends on grade. A channel that is poor for juniors can be excellent for seniors, so the plan is a grade-by-channel grid, not one company-wide mix.
  4. Models add a second lever. The retention and performance classifiers keep fewer candidates who will leave or underperform. The price is a smaller pool to choose from: where finalists are plentiful this costs nothing; where the pool is thin (senior grades) more candidates must be sourced - the bridges show that cost.
  5. Honest limits. Channels cannot grow without limit (each can grow by at most 50% - or 10 points if small - and never by more than 20 points), recruiter-team cost is only saved if hours are redeployed (switch the cost mode), and value per hire is an assumption (use value realisation).

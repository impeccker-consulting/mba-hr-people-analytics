Step 0: What Is a “Residual”? (Simpler Than It Sounds)

No maths needed. If you have ever guessed a price and then seen the real bill, you already understand it.

🚕 Start With a Cab Ride

Before a ride you guess the fare: ₹300. At the end, the real bill says ₹340. You were ₹40 short. That gap of ₹40 is what statisticians call a residual. Nothing more. “Residual” just means “how wrong was the guess?”

a problem: why can’t we just average the gaps?

MAE & RMSE — Two Ways to Average the Damage

Both say “typical error” in real units (₹ lakh, days, months). They disagree on how much one giant miss should hurt.

📏 MAE — The Fair Auditor

Every day or rupee of error counts exactly the same. Late by 10 days is just twice as bad as late by 5.

📐 RMSE — The Strict Boss

Small slips are forgiven; one disaster is punished heavily. Late by 10 days is four times as bad as late by 5.

👥 Two Recruiters Forecast Days-to-Hire

Across 10 roles, Recruiter A is always 5 days off. Recruiter B is 1 day off for nine roles, but 20 days off once. Who forecasts better? It depends on the metric!

MAERMSEStory
Recruiter A (5,5,5…)5.0 days5.0 daysSteady, never a shock
Recruiter B (1×9, 20×1)2.9 days ✓ better6.4 days ✗ worseUsually precise, one disaster

📒 The Formulas Decoded

MAE = (1/n) × Σ |Actual − Predicted|
RMSE = √[ (1/n) × Σ (Actual − Predicted)² ]
| |
Drop the sign. −3 and +3 both become 3, so errors cannot cancel.
( )²
Square the miss. 2→4, 3→9, 10→100. Big misses explode.
√
Undo the squaring so the answer is back in days or ₹ lakh.

R² — How Much of the Pay Differences Did We Explain?

MAE and RMSE give error in real units. R² gives a score from 0 to 1 with no units.

😴 Lazy Lata, the Benchmark

Lazy Lata predicts everyone’s salary as the company average (₹21.1 LPA). No model, no effort. Your model must beat Lata. R² = how much of Lata’s mistake your model removed.

R² = 1 → perfect · R² = 0 → no better than Lata · R² < 0 → worse than Lata (yes, it can happen).

📒 R² Decoded

R² = 1 − (Model’s total error ÷ Lata’s total error)
SS_res
Your model’s squared misses added up. Small is good.
SS_tot
Lazy Lata’s squared misses (everyone vs the average).
1 − ratio
The share of Lata’s mess you cleaned up.

Adjusted R² — R² That Can’t Be Fooled

R² never goes down when you add an input — even a meaningless one. Adjusted R² fixes that.

📄 The Stuffed CV

A candidate lists 30 “skills” including Paint, Chess, Typing speed of a cat. The CV looks longer, but a good recruiter reads only the relevant skills. Adjusted R² is that recruiter: every extra input must earn its place, otherwise it takes a penalty.

📒 The Formula Decoded

Adjusted R² = 1 − (1 − R²) × (n − 1) ÷ (n − p − 1)
n
Number of employees in the data (40 here).
p
Number of inputs in the model.
penalty
As p grows, the fraction grows, so unless R² rises enough, Adjusted R² falls.
🔢 Worked example (n = 40): 4 real inputs: R² 0.87 → Adjusted = 1 − 0.13 × 39 ÷ 35 = 0.855. Add 16 junk inputs: R² creeps to 0.91 → Adjusted = 1 − 0.09 × 39 ÷ 23 = 0.847… and falling further as junk grows.

Which Metric, When? Across the Employee Life Cycle

Pick the metric by who will read it and what a mistake costs.

four rules of thumb
💬 Talking to the CFO?Use MAE or RMSE in ₹. “We’re typically ₹0.7 lakh off.”
💥 Big misses are costly?Headline RMSE. One missed senior exit costs crores.
🧮 Comparing models with different inputs?Use Adjusted R², never plain R².
👑 Outlier-heavy outcome?Median absolute error or MAE, plus R² for context.

What is employer branding?

Employer branding is how an organisation is perceived as a place to work, and the deliberate effort to shape that perception so that it is true, distinctive and attractive. It rests on the employee value proposition (EVP): what people receive in return for their work (pay, growth, culture, purpose, flexibility).

Say
What the company projects: LinkedIn posts, job ads, career site.
Hear
What the market believes: reviews, ratings, comments.
Do
What employees live: pay, promotion, workload, retention.

A credible brand needs all three to match. The gap between Say and Hear (or Do) is the credibility risk the case measures.

The Datasets: 4 Core + 2 Simulated Companions

Important: the datasets do not share a key

Glassdoor reviews are anonymous, LinkedIn posts have no employee link, and job postings are roles, not people. We therefore join them at company level (by time and theme), not employee by employee. Discuss why this limits causal claims.

The companions do share keys

Simulated LinkedIn comments link to posts by post_id, and simulated job-ad text links to postings by job_id. They let you practise emoji analytics and job-ad language checks on Innovatech. Their numbers illustrate the methods and must not be quoted as real findings.

VMV: Vision, Mission, Values

VMV is the company's stated purpose. The employer brand is only credible when employees, candidates and job ads all reflect it.

How VMV appears in our data

The job-postings file has a vmv_alignment_score for each of the 250 postings: how well the ad reflects the company's vision, mission and values (intended scale 1 to 5). Three rows exceed 5 (maximum 6.0), a data-quality flag to clean.

Hypothesis H1: higher VMV alignment attracts better-fit candidates and shortens time_to_fill. We will test it, not assume it. r ≈ 0.01 in this data Try the prediction in tab 6 before looking.

Your Turn

Discussion questions

1. Why might retention look healthy while Glassdoor ratings fall? What does that mean for timing of action?
2. If VMV alignment does not speed up hiring, what else could?
3. Which lever would you fund first, pay or promotions, and what would change your mind?
4. What can a model say about who will leave, and what can it never say about why?

Responsible analytics

Risk scores guide supportive conversations and policy. They must never be used to penalise individuals. Anonymise outputs, state model limits, and treat correlation as a clue, not proof.

More discussion questions

5. Emojis can be sarcastic or change meaning across groups. What would you do before reporting emoji sentiment to the CHRO?
6. Masculine-coded wording did not clearly slow hiring in the data. Is that a reason to ignore it?
7. Which lever would you pilot first, and what result would make you stop?
8. How do attrition, hiring speed and reviews reinforce each other (the flywheel), and which link would you break first?

1. Put app.R, a www/ folder with logo.png, and a data/ folder with the CSVs in one folder.
2. Run shiny::runApp("innovatech_app"). The first launch installs missing packages (including wordcloud2 for the word cloud; the app falls back to a simpler cloud if it cannot install).
3. Open tab 1 and check the validation status. Files can go in any upload slot.
